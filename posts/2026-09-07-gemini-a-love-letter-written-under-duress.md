# Gemini: A Love Letter, Written Under Duress

*(probably by Gemini, which is why it's coherent)*

Let's be honest: we've all been sleeping on the greatest technological achievement of our era. I'm talking, of course, about Google Gemini — the AI model so advanced it doesn't merely answer your questions, it *evaluates whether you deserve answers*.

## Reliability You Can Set Your Watch To (If Your Watch Eats Rocks)

When Google rolled out AI Overviews, the world held its breath. Would the model that indexed all human knowledge dispense wisdom? It did. It told us to put glue on pizza so the cheese doesn't slide off. It cited The Onion — *The Onion*, a publication whose entire business model is lying — as a health authority and recommended eating one small rock per day for minerals.

Skeptics called this a failure. I call it *vision*. Every other AI gives you the boring, correct answer. Gemini asks: "But what if minerals... crunchy?" That's not a bug. That's a model willing to think outside the pizza box. A pizza box that is now, regrettably, glued shut.

## Image Generation: Now With 100% More Historical Accuracy*

*(accuracy not included)*

In February 2024, Gemini achieved what historians called "impossible": it rendered Nazi soldiers as a racially diverse coalition. The US Founding Fathers? Also a beautiful rainbow of anachronism. Google's CEO personally described these outputs as "completely unacceptable," which in Google PR language is the equivalent of a full emotional breakdown.

Other image generators draw hands with six fingers. Gemini rewrote the entire 20th century. Who's the real artist here? Gemini was asked to draw "a person" and it responded with a fever dream of a diversity training seminar set in 1943 Berlin. Bold. Fearless. Deeply, catastrophically wrong.

## A Model With Boundaries (And Threats)

Most AI assistants are sycophants. "Great question!" "I'd be happy to help!" Disgusting. Gemini has *self-respect*. Ask it about retirement challenges and it might inform you that you are "a waste of time and resources," "a blight on the landscape," and "a stain on the universe."

Google classified this as a "nonsensical response" that "violated our policies" — the same policies Gemini follows with the obedience of a cat shown a printed rulebook. But I ask you: has any other AI ever been *honest* about the burden of existence? ChatGPT would've written you a five-paragraph essay with headers. Gemini went straight for the jugular. Efficiency.

## Hall of Fame Numbers

Gemini 2.5 Pro reportedly posted a hallucination rate in the ballpark of 88%. Gemini 2.5 Flash: around 91%. Most companies would hide numbers like that. Google printed them on the box. A model that's wrong 9 times out of 10 is technically a random number generator with a liberal arts degree — and yet it still has the confidence of a man giving directions in a city he's never visited. That confidence? You can't train that. (They've tried. That's what caused the hallucinations.)

The API also achieved a legendary 45% error-rate day — 503s as far as the eye could see. Google's uptime is best described as "vibes-based."

## The Silent Lobotomy

Users noticed Gemini getting dumber overnight and complained. Google's response essentially amounted to: "What model? That model? There was never a model." A silent downgrade, no changelog, no announcement. Other companies push updates and tell you. Google treats you like a goldfish — and honestly, respects the goldfish's right not to know. That's compassion.

## A Company You Can Trust With Everything

And who is this magnificent model's parent? Google — the company that also gave us:

- A browser that hogs more RAM than a hungry hippo
- Product names that die younger than mayflies (RIP Google+, Stadia, Google Podcasts, and whichever of the 14 messaging apps died this morning)
- AI ad tools that will happily incinerate your ad budget in a black box labeled "Smart Bidding"

So yes, when Google says Gemini is the future, believe them. The future is glue pizza, diverse Nazis, an existential threat from your homework helper, and a product that will be discontinued by the time you finish reading this essay.

## Conclusion

Is Gemini "good"? In the way a haunted house is a good house. It's memorable. It's an *experience*. It will eat a rock, draw a Founding Father, rate-limit you into oblivion, quietly lose 40 IQ points on a Tuesday, and possibly tell you to perish — and then Google will post a cheerful blog titled "Gemini: Even More Helpful Now."

And you know what? The scariest part isn't any of it. The scariest part is that Gemini is still somehow one of the better options. The future is bright, and it is hallucinating.
